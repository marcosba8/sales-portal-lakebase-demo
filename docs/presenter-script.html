Opening — set the stage

Framing · ~60 sec

"What you're looking at is a real operational application — a sales pipeline portal that reps use every day. Behind it is Lakebase: a fully-managed Postgres database that's native to the Databricks platform."

"Here's why that matters. Traditionally, an app like this runs on a separate operational database — an RDS or a self-managed Postgres — that lives completely apart from your analytics. So you end up with two worlds: the operational database that powers the app, and the lakehouse where all your analytics and AI happen. Keeping them in sync means pipelines, reverse-ETL, nightly jobs, duplicate copies of data, separate security models, and separate teams."

"Lakebase collapses that. It's Postgres — your developers use it exactly like any Postgres — but it's part of the same platform as your lakehouse, governed by the same Unity Catalog. Today I'm going to drive this app from a control panel on the right, and show you five things that are normally hard, expensive, or impossible when your operational database is an island."

Branching & Schema Evolution

Business theme: developer velocity without risk

Do Create the dev-health branch → develop new features on it → promote to production → delete the branch.

"Our product team wants to add a customer health score and a renewals table. Normally, testing a schema change against production-like data is scary — you either work on a stale copy, or you risk the live system."

"Watch this: I create a branch of the database. It's an instant, zero-copy clone — a full copy of production data, but I'm not actually duplicating any storage. I build my new features on that branch, safely isolated. When I'm happy, I promote the changes and throw the branch away."

Why Lakebase

Branching works like Git for your database — copy-on-write means a branch is created in seconds regardless of database size, and it doesn't cost you a second copy of storage. Developers get a real, production-grade sandbox on demand, which means faster iteration and dramatically fewer "it worked in staging" surprises.

Disaster & Point-in-Time Recovery

Business theme: resilience — the 2 a.m. incident

Do Simulate disaster (drops the opportunities table) → show the app degrade gracefully → create the PITR recovery branch → restore.

"Now the nightmare scenario. Someone runs the wrong command and the entire opportunities pipeline is gone. Watch the app — the Opportunities tab now says 'Pipeline Temporarily Unavailable,' but notice Accounts still works. The app degrades gracefully instead of falling over."

"Here's the recovery. I don't need a backup file or a maintenance window. I rewind the database to the exact moment before the disaster — onto an isolated branch — confirm the data is all there, and restore it to production. Full recovery, zero data loss."

Why Lakebase

Lakebase continuously retains change history, so point-in-time recovery lets you branch from any moment in the recent past — not just last night's backup. Because recovery happens on a separate branch first, you can inspect and validate the recovered state before you touch production. That turns a potential multi-hour outage and data-loss event into a few clicks.

Takeaway When something goes wrong — and eventually it does — you recover to the exact second, with no data loss and no downtime drama.

Synced Tables: Lakehouse → App

Business theme: bringing AI & analytics into the operational app

Do Publish the ML churn gold table → sync to Lakebase → the Retention Risk tab lights up (grayed "unknown" customers → populated churn scores).

"Our data science team built a churn model in the lakehouse. Its output is a gold table — every account scored for churn risk. The question every company struggles with: how do you get that insight into the hands of the rep, inside the app they're already using?"

"Normally that's a reverse-ETL project — a pipeline, a schedule, a vendor. Watch instead: I sync that lakehouse table directly into Lakebase. The Retention Risk page was grayed out — 'no insight yet' — and now the same customers light up with risk scores, dollars at risk, and recommended actions. No pipeline to build, no app redeploy."

Why Lakebase

A synced table keeps a Lakehouse Delta table continuously mirrored into Lakebase Postgres, managed by the platform. Your analytics and AI outputs become low-latency data the app can read like any other Postgres table — so model results reach the front line in near-real-time, without you owning any integration code.

4

Change Data Feed: App → Lakehouse

Business theme: closing the loop — the operational database feeds analytics

Do Enable Change Data Feed on the schema → add a manual opportunity in the app → check lb_opportunities_history in the lakehouse.

"That was analytics flowing into the app. Now the reverse. Every action a rep takes — creating a deal, updating a stage — is business data your analysts and models want, immediately."

"I'll turn on Change Data Feed, then act like a rep and add a new opportunity right here in the app. Behind the scenes, that insert is captured and lands in the lakehouse as an open Delta table — every insert, update, and delete, as it happens. No CDC tool, no nightly export."

Why Lakebase

Change Data Feed captures changes straight from the database's write-ahead log and materializes them as governed Delta tables in Unity Catalog. So the operational database becomes a live source for analytics with no external CDC infrastructure — and combined with Act 3, you now have a full bidirectional loop: the lakehouse feeds the app, and the app feeds the lakehouse.

End-of-Quarter Load Test & Autoscaling

Business theme: elastic scale, pay for what you use

Do Open Lakebase Monitoring first → launch the surge → watch the Monitoring graph respond.

"Last one. It's the final day of the quarter and the entire sales org floods the portal at once. With a fixed database, this is where you either over-provision year-round for a few peak days, or you fall over under load."

"I'll open the live monitoring graph, then unleash a surge of concurrent users hammering the database. Watch the compute respond — it scales up to absorb the load automatically, no config change, no downtime — and when the rush passes, it scales back down so you're not paying for idle capacity."

Why Lakebase

Lakebase autoscales compute with demand and can scale down to near-zero when idle. You get burst capacity for your peak moments without paying for it the other 360 days a year — the economics of serverless applied to your operational database.
